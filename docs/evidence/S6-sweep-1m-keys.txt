What the number covers, and what it does not
--------------------------------------------

Included in the timed region: iterating the settled-key set, the batched
pipelined reads (500 keys per round trip), the per-key comparison, and
categorisation of any disagreement.

Excluded: seeding both the store and the oracle, which happens before
ResetTimer; and confirmation, which by construction happens a settlement
window after a sweep and is not part of a sweep's latency.

The oracle and the store are seeded to AGREE. This is deliberate. A sweep
that found a million divergences would spend its time allocating findings
and would be measuring the reporting path rather than the read path, which
is not what S6 is about. The benchmark asserts `len(warm.Findings) == 0`
before the timer starts, so a seeding mistake fails the run rather than
quietly producing a faster number.

A trap this benchmark walked into, and the fix
----------------------------------------------

The first version configured the oracle with `MaxTrackedKeys: sweepKeys + 1`
and asserted that a million keys went in and a million were tracked. It
failed:

    Error:    Not equal:
              expected: 1000000
              actual  : 996673
    Messages: oracle did not retain the whole keyspace

That is D-003 reproducing itself exactly as documented. The key budget is
enforced per shard so that eviction stays shard-local; hashing a million
keys across 64 shards leaves some shards over-subscribed while others sit
idle, and every key landing on a full shard is evicted while the oracle is
globally not full. The loss here was 3,327 keys, 0.33% — consistent with
the 3,115 keys and 0.31% recorded in D-003 during Phase 1.

D-003 predicted this specific wrong turn: "The natural benchmark asserts a
million keys go in and a million keys are tracked, and that assertion fails
— which reads as an eviction bug. Chasing a correctness bug that is really
a statistical property of sharding is the expensive kind of wrong turn."

The fix is the documented recommendation: allow headroom. The benchmark now
configures 1.1M for a 1M keyspace, and the comment at that line says why and
points at D-003, so the next person to read it does not have to rediscover
the reasoning.

Allocation note
---------------

9,048,053 allocations per sweep is roughly 9 per key. §16.8's allocation
budget of "< 5 allocs/key" applies to BenchmarkGetMany500, the batched read
in isolation, and is met there. A full sweep additionally allocates for
settled-key iteration and per-key comparison. D-007 records why the read
path cannot go below ~4 allocs/key: that is the go-redis client's own floor
for a pipelined command, not driftwatch's.
