Evidence for docs/DISCOVERIES.md D-006.

Question: PRD section 9 M8 warns that a FLUSHDB during a SCAN restarts the
cursor at 0 and spins the iteration forever. Does that reproduce?

Method: seed 10,000 keys, scan with COUNT 100, destroy the keyspace at
various points, and record how the iteration ends. Gives up at 200,000
calls rather than hanging. Run against real Redis 6.2 and 7.2 in Docker.

$ docker run -d --name dw-redis6 -p 16379:6379 redis:6.2-alpine
$ docker run -d --name dw-redis7 -p 17379:6379 redis:7.2-alpine
$ ./scanprobe 127.0.0.1:16379 'Redis 6'
$ ./scanprobe 127.0.0.1:17379 'Redis 7'

=== Redis 6 (6.2.23) ===
baseline           : 100 calls, 10000 keys seen, 100 distinct cursors, terminated
FLUSHDB at call 1  : 2 calls, 100 keys seen, 2 distinct cursors, terminated
FLUSHDB + refill   : 100 calls, 10010 keys seen, 100 distinct cursors, terminated
FLUSHDB every call : 51 calls, 5111 keys seen, 51 distinct cursors, terminated

=== Redis 7 (7.2.15) ===
baseline           : 100 calls, 10000 keys seen, 100 distinct cursors, terminated
FLUSHDB at call 1  : 2 calls, 101 keys seen, 2 distinct cursors, terminated
FLUSHDB + refill   : 100 calls, 10002 keys seen, 100 distinct cursors, terminated
FLUSHDB every call : 51 calls, 5070 keys seen, 51 distinct cursors, terminated

Answer: it does not loop. Every case terminated, and no cursor was ever
returned twice, on either version.

What happens instead: the scan terminates EARLY and reports success.
'FLUSHDB at call 1' finished after 2 calls having seen 100 of the 10,000
keys - one percent of the keyspace - with a cursor of 0 and no error.
A caller cannot distinguish that from a keyspace that genuinely holds 100
keys.
