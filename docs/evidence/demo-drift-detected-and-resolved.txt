# `make demo` — drift detected, then resolved

Captured 2026-08-01 against the compose stack in `deploy/demo/`. Reproduce with:

    make demo                 # wait ~60s for the oracle to fill
    make demo-inject-drift
    open http://localhost:3000

Everything below is real: a ZMQ PUB publisher over TCP, a materializer that is a
separate process subscribing to it, a real Redis, and driftwatch as a third
independent subscriber. Nothing shares memory with anything else.

## Steady state before the fault

    driftwatch_coverage_ratio          0.9969
    driftwatch_divergent_keys (all)    0
    driftwatch_oracle_keys{trust=complete}  ~19,500
    events applied                     ~400/sec

## The fault

    $ make demo-inject-drift
    demo-inject-drift: Redis holds 19489 keys; deleting 400 of them
    demo-inject-drift: Redis now holds 19097 keys (392 removed)

392 keys deleted straight out of Redis, behind driftwatch's back. That models a
materializer that failed a batch, an eviction, or an operator mistake — from
driftwatch's side all three look the same.

## What the numbers say

**Detection took one sweep plus one confirmation.** The fault landed at 10:14:56
and 360 keys were confirmed by 10:15:03. Not instant, and deliberately so: a key
seen to disagree once is a candidate, not a finding, and it has to still
disagree on a second read a settlement window later. That is the mechanism that
makes the number trustworthy enough to alert on.

**360 of 392, not 392.** The other 32 were touched by the publisher again
between the two reads, so they had already been rewritten by the time the
confirming read happened. Those are counted as transient rather than confirmed —
which is correct. They were never divergent by the time anybody could have acted
on it.

**Coverage never moved.** It sat between 0.9969 and 0.9999 across the whole
episode, which is what makes the divergence count mean something: driftwatch was
asserting on essentially the entire keyspace the whole time. A count of 360
against a coverage of 0.03 would have been noise.

**Recovery was not instrumented.** Nothing told driftwatch the keys had come
back. The publisher kept emitting, the materializer kept writing, and each key
resolved on the first sweep after an event touched it again — which is the
`drift_resolved_total` counter climbing to 210 while `drift_episodes_total`
stayed at 360.
